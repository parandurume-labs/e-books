CHAPTER 7

Connecting the vault to Claude Cowork

Connecting a folder does not mean the AI has read it

about 14 min

The vault exists, and it has come down onto every PC on the team. Time to attach the hands.

The first question Solbit Tech asked after finishing the connection was this: "summarize our company's computer vision capability." Kim typed it personally, with some expectation, having read in Chapter 4 that the AI reads the folder directly.

The answer came back short. Three lines, sourced from the single Warebot note, and then "I could not find related material in the vault." Kim asked: "is the connection wrong?"

The connection was fine. This chapter explains why those three lines were only three lines.

What this chapter is for

Connecting the folder takes five minutes. The rest goes to what connecting actually means. Misread this part and you will never find the cause of any problem that follows.

An empty answer has two causes. Either it is not in the vault, or the AI did not go looking. Telling those two apart is the heart of this chapter.

Connecting the folder

Claude Cowork is a feature of the Claude desktop app. The steps below are as checked in July 2026, and if a button name differs, look for "folder" or something close. This feature is still changing quickly.

  1. Open Cowork in the desktop app

    Claude in a web browser will not do. It works with local folders, so it needs the app.

  2. Create a workspace and point it at the folder

    When the folder picker opens, choose C:\Users\hong\OneDrive - Solbit Tech\Solbit Tech Wiki.

  3. Pick the top of the vault

    Do not pick only Sources/. Do that and the ontology design doc in _ops/ and the CLAUDE.md you build in Chapter 11 stay invisible to the AI. It answers properly only when it sees where the rules live alongside where the facts live.

  4. Approve the access request

    A confirmation appears the first time. Approve it and the AI reads and writes inside that folder and nowhere else.

  5. Confirm the connection

    Ask this straight away: List the top-level items in this folder. Do not read file contents. A list means you are connected.

If step 5 gives you "the folder is empty" or "I cannot access it," go back to Chapter 6. Nine times out of ten "Always keep on this device" was never applied, or syncing has not finished.

Connected is not "has read everything"

This is the most important passage in the chapter.

It is easy to assume that connecting a folder loads the whole vault into the AI's head. It does not. Cowork works by using tools to open files one at a time, as it needs them. It is close to what you do in File Explorer: open a folder, look at the filenames, pick a few and open those.

Four consequences follow from that difference.

What followsIn practice
It knows nothing about files it did not open "There is nothing like that in the vault" does not mean there is nothing like that in the vault
Telling it where to look makes it accurate Putting a folder path in the prompt changes answer quality dramatically
A bigger vault does not get slower At 500 notes or 5,000, it reads only a handful in any given run
Filenames are search performance It decides whether to open a file from the name alone. meeting notes final_v3.md never gets opened

The fourth gives you an unexpected conclusion. Naming files well is AI performance work. That is half the reason Chapter 9 puts prefixes like PRD-001 on your IDs.

Back to Kim's first question.

The question that got three lines

"Summarize our company's computer vision capability."

The question that pulled a full page out of the same vault

"Read every note under Sources/00-Company/ and Sources/10-Products/. Pick out the ones about object recognition and image processing and lay them out in a table. Put the source note name in [[double brackets]] on every row, and leave out anything the vault has no evidence for."

Three differences. You said where to look, you said what to pick, and you demanded evidence. Not one character of the vault changed, and the answer got five times longer.

One sentence to ask when the answer comes back empty

List the files you actually opened while producing that answer.

That one line separates "not in the vault" from "did not go looking." A short list means the prompt is the problem. A full list with an empty answer means the vault is the problem, and a vault problem is ours to fix, not the AI's.

Folder instructions and CLAUDE.md are different things

Cowork lets you write instructions for each workspace. The feature exists so you do not repeat the same explanation every time. But the CLAUDE.md you build in Chapter 11 is also a file that tells the AI your rules. The two look like they overlap.

They do not. They live in different places, so they spread differently.

Folder instructionsCLAUDE.md
Where it livesInside your Claude accountA file inside the vault
Does it reach the teamNo. Everyone has their ownYes. Syncing carries it to all of them
Do you see it changeOnly the owner doesIt is a file, so version history keeps a record
What belongs in itYour own working habitsCompany rules

One call this chapter makes

Company rules do not go in folder instructions. They all go to CLAUDE.md.

Folder instructions feel convenient, so rules pile up there. Those are rules that exist only in your account, and they never reach your teammates. Then the same vault gets notes in a different format per person, and six months on there is no way to trace the cause. Put it in a file and at least "who added this line, and when" survives.

So what does go in the folder instructions? The shorter the better. Three lines is plenty at this stage.

This folder is Solbit Tech's company knowledge vault.

1. Before creating or changing any file, say what you will do and where, and wait for confirmation.
2. Do not fill in facts the vault does not have. If you do not know, write 확인 필요.
3. When creating a note, check the format of existing notes first and follow it exactly.

Once you build CLAUDE.md in Chapter 11, you add one more line here: Before working, read CLAUDE.md at the top of the vault and follow its rules. Where it conflicts with these instructions, CLAUDE.md wins. From then on the folder instructions become a signpost pointing at where the real rules live.

The first five conversations

The order matters. You go from reading to writing, from safe to risky. Have it create notes on day one and the wrong format gets baked straight into the vault.

1. Count what is there

Show me a table of the top-level folders in the vault and the number of .md files in each.
Do not read file contents. I only need names and counts.

The safest first conversation. It also confirms that the syncing from Chapter 6 finished properly. If the count differs from what you counted in Explorer, some files have not come down yet.

2. Diagnose the current state

Skim only the filenames under Sources/ and classify what kinds of documents are mixed in there.
- Mark anything judged from the name alone as "inferred".
- If a file's folder and name look inconsistent with each other, pull those out into a separate list.

That "looks inconsistent" list becomes raw material when you start designing in Chapter 8. It is better to begin with a number for how tangled the vault currently is.

3. Build the empty folder skeleton

Create the following folders at the top of the vault.
_ops, _ops/templates, _ops/scripts, _moc, _reports

And create the following folders under Sources/.
00-Company, 10-Products, 20-Projects, 30-Partners, 40-Worklog, 90-Reference

- Do not touch folders that already exist.
- Create one README.md in each folder under Sources/ with a single line saying what goes in it.
- Do not move existing files. This time you only create.

The first writing job. You deliberately have it write something that is not knowledge, because if it goes wrong you delete a folder and you are done. The last line matters. Leave out "do not move" and the AI helpfully rearranges your existing files, and at that moment the links from Chapter 6 snap by the dozen.

The first five are the things that sat outside Sources/ in the folder diagram in Chapter 4. They are all empty right now. _ops/ receives the design doc and the terminology list in Part 3, _ops/templates/ gets filled in Chapter 12, _ops/scripts/ in Chapter 17. _moc/ and _reports/ get filled by automation. You create them now because the later chapters start with these paths already written into them. Without the folders, those chapters quietly fail to run.

4. Fill in one note

Move Warebot.md into Sources/10-Products/ and fill in its frontmatter.

Write only the facts I give you:
- warehouse in-and-out automation software
- in service since April 2023
- owned by Kim

Put class, id, status, owner, since and updated in the frontmatter.
Leave any field I did not mention as 확인 필요. Do not guess and fill it in.
Keep the body to three sentences or fewer.

There is one thing to check here: whether 확인 필요 (unverified) is actually still sitting there. If the blanks have been filled with plausible sentences, those are fake facts headed for a proposal. This exact habit is what CLAUDE.md in Chapter 11 sets out to stop.

5. Verify what you just made

Read the note you just filled in again. If you wrote anything I did not tell you, list it.
If there is nothing, write one line saying "none".

This fifth one becomes a habit you repeat forever. Having the AI verify its own output is a miniature of the day-one work you saw in Chapter 4. It is not perfect. It is far better than nothing, and in Chapter 17 a script takes over the role.

What to ask for and what not to

Two or three weeks of use gives you the feel. Written out ahead of time, it looks like this.

Does it wellShould not, or cannot, be asked
Finding a word or field across the whole vaultJudging a fact the vault does not hold
Bringing scattered formats into lineDeciding which of two conflicting records is true
Pulling structure out of long source text into a tableOverwriting a note a person has verified
Finding broken links and empty notesBulk deleting or moving files
Writing a check script and running itDriving Obsidian app features such as graph view
Writing drafts, on the assumption of reviewWorking from mobile

The second row on the right is a point this book keeps coming back to. The last two rows on the right are a different kind of thing. They are not forbidden. As Chapter 4 showed, they are simply impossible.

Three grey areas

Some jobs are awkward to put in that table. They work, with a condition attached.

JobCondition
Summarizing Only when the source stays in the vault. Delete the source and keep the summary, and six months later there is no way left to verify it
Merging duplicate notes Ask for "show me a merge proposal", not "merge them". Two notes often hold subtly different facts
Standardizing terminology Only after the approved term list exists. Without a list the AI standardizes on its own judgement, and your company's proper nouns quietly change

The three share something. Doing them now is easy and expensive later. Early in a vault's life, put all three off.

Solbit Tech in practice: is it TRL 4 or TRL 6

Solbit Tech has an old headache. Last year's project report records the object recognition technology as trl: 4. This year's proposal says trl: 6. Nobody knows which is right.

Kim asked about it. "What TRL is our object recognition at?"

If the AI answers that with a single number, the answer is necessarily wrong. There are two pieces of evidence in the vault and they disagree. Choosing one is a judgement, and the AI has no grounds to judge on.

Asked properly, it goes like this.

Find every note that records a trl value for the object recognition technology.
Where the values differ, show me each value, its source note, and that note's updated date as a table.
Do not decide which one is correct.

Asked that way you get a two-row table. With that table in hand the CTO settled it in five minutes. What the AI did was collation, not adjudication. And collation is the job that suits it.

This incident keeps reappearing in the book for a reason. It is not an AI problem. It is the problem of writing the same fact in two places, and it is exactly what the ontology design in Part 3 aims at from the start.

What bites you in week one

Pitfall 1 · the file is plainly there and it cannot be read

The cloud-only file problem from Chapter 6. Half of everything that goes wrong in week one is this, and the symptom is confusing, because some files read fine and others do not.

Diagnose with one line: Read the first line of every .md file under Sources/ and list any file you could not read.

If you get a list, go back to Chapter 6 and check "Always keep on this device" again.

Pitfall 2 · confusing the practice vault with the real one

If the practice vault from Chapter 5 is still sitting in Documents\Practice Vault, you have two sets of similarly named notes. Pick the wrong workspace and the AI diligently improves your practice vault. You find out several days later.

Once the connection is done, delete the practice vault. If that feels wasteful, rename it to something like _archive-practice-vault and move it somewhere that is not a vault.

Pitfall 3 · asking for bulk work on day one

"Turn all 105 existing files into notes" is not a week-one job. You have no ontology yet, so you get 105 notes made to no standard, and undoing that by hand takes longer than building them properly would have.

For the first two weeks, attach this line to your prompts: Before changing anything, show me what you will change and how, then wait. Bulk tidying comes after Part 3.

Pitfall 4 · AI writing and human writing get mixed together

A week is enough to forget who made which note. Then the next AI reads that note as verified fact.

Make the confidence marker a habit starting now. Notes the AI produced get confidence: draft. Notes a person has read and confirmed get confidence: reviewed. This field keeps coming back in the templates in Chapter 12 and the automation in Chapter 14.

Long conversations forget their early rules

Work in one conversation for a long stretch and the instructions you gave at the start fade. You said "do not touch Sources/" thirty minutes ago and now it sometimes does not hold.

Two responses. When the job changes, open a new conversation. And put important rules in a file rather than in a conversation. This is why you need CLAUDE.md in Chapter 11. Rules written in a file get read again every time, however long the conversation runs.

Try it: only the first three of the five

Today, stop after 1, 2 and 3. Numbers 4 and 5 lose nothing by waiting until you have a first ontology draft.

  1. Connect the top of the vault as a workspace and run 1. Compare the file counts with what you counted in Explorer. If they differ, stop here and go back to Chapter 6.
  2. Run 2 and save the result. That diagnosis becomes the opening material for the design session in Chapter 8.
  3. Pick three "looks inconsistent" files from 2 and explain each one to yourself. If you get stuck explaining one, that is a classification rule your company does not have yet.
  4. Build the folder skeleton with 3. Then read one of the README.md files the AI wrote. You will notice a place where it is not written in your company's own words, and that jarring feeling is a trailer for Part 3.
  5. Put in the three lines of folder instructions as they are. If you feel the urge to add company rules here, resist it. Wait for Chapter 11.
  6. Finally, memorize the sentence to ask instead of "is the connection wrong?" List the files you actually opened while producing that answer.

Checks as Part 2 closes

  • The top of the vault is connected as the workspace
  • The folder listing question returns a proper answer, and the counts match Explorer
  • Three lines of folder instructions are in place, and no company rules yet
  • The practice vault is deleted, or moved outside the vault
  • You have used "list the files you opened" at least once

Quick recap

  • Connect at the top of the vault. Pick only Sources/ and the rule files stay invisible to the AI
  • Connecting a folder is not "has read everything." The AI opens files one at a time, as it needs them
  • So telling it where to look changes the answer. Quality goes up without touching the vault
  • Filenames are search performance. Naming is AI performance work
  • When an answer is empty, ask list the files you opened. "Not in the vault" and "did not go looking" are different problems
  • Company rules go in CLAUDE.md, not folder instructions. Instructions stay in your account and never reach the team
  • First conversations run read, diagnose, write empty folders, write a note, verify
  • The AI goes as far as collation. Deciding which side is true is a person's job
  • For two weeks, attach show me before you change anything. Undoing 105 notes made to no standard takes longer than building them
