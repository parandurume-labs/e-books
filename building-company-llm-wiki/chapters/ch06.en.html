CHAPTER 6

Connecting the whole team through a Teams shared folder

The single most important click in this book is in here

about 13 min

The vault you made in Chapter 5 still lives only on your laptop. Leave it there and it is a well-organized personal notepad. Personal notepads run fine for about three months and then go quiet the week their owner takes a holiday.

At Solbit Tech one sentence marked the spot. Park, heading into the Daesung Logistics meeting, asked: "that summary of the Warebot track record, could you send it over?"

The moment "send it over" appears, the system has already failed. Sending creates a copy, the copy differs from the original two days later, and at the next meeting someone asks again which one is current. The fragmentation diagnosed in Chapter 1 reappears inside your shiny new folder.

What this chapter is for

You turn the vault into a folder the whole team sees at once, and you deal with the three problems that come with it up front: cloud-only files, edit conflicts, and the boundary around confidential material.

The procedure itself takes twenty minutes. The rest of the chapter is the things that will definitely bite you if you skip them.

Putting the vault in a Teams shared folder

As settled in Chapter 4, Teams and OneDrive handle delivery and nothing else. All you have to do is make one folder and get it down onto everyone's PC.

  1. Pick one team in Teams

    A new team works, an existing one works. What matters is that the people who see the vault and the members of the team are the same set. That is your entire permission boundary from here on. No separate accounts, no separate permission settings.

  2. Open the channel's Files tab

    Every Teams channel has one file storage area attached. Underneath it is a SharePoint document library, and you can use it perfectly well without knowing that.

  3. Click "Add shortcut to OneDrive"

    As of July 2026 it sits at the top of the Files tab. If the name differs, look for "shortcut" or something close. Clicking it makes that folder appear inside your OneDrive and sync automatically to your PC.

  4. Check the path in File Explorer

    The folder shows up under C:\Users\hong\OneDrive - Solbit Tech\. This is the path that keeps coming back through the rest of the book.

  5. Make the vault folder inside it

    Solbit Tech named theirs Solbit Tech Wiki. The full path becomes C:\Users\hong\OneDrive - Solbit Tech\Solbit Tech Wiki.

  6. In Obsidian, use "Open folder as vault" and point it there

    If you made notes in the Chapter 5 practice vault, copy them in through File Explorer first, then open it. Do not copy the .obsidian folder. The new vault builds its own.

  7. Each teammate repeats steps 3 and 6 on their own PC

    These two steps repeat once per person. In exchange, once is enough.

There is also a "Sync" button on the Files tab. Either one brings the files down. What differs is where the folder lands.

MethodPath you get on the PCWhat this book picks
Add shortcut to OneDriveOneDrive - CompanyName\FolderNameRecommended. Short path, and several teams still collect in one place
SyncCompanyName\TeamName - ChannelNameIt works. The channel name goes into the path, so it gets long

There is a reason to care about path length. This path goes into scheduled task prompts, into Python scripts, and into the instructions you hand teammates, over and over. Shorter means fewer typos.

The single most important click in this book

Get this far and every file is visible. Obsidian opens them fine. Then you connect the folder to Claude Cowork in that state and this happens.

You

"Read every note in the vault folder and make me a list."

Claude

"I could not find any readable files in that folder."

While Explorer sits there showing you the notes. This is the trap Chapter 4 warned you about.

The core pitfall · a visible file and a present file are different things

By default OneDrive keeps only the filename on the PC and leaves the contents in the cloud. The feature exists to save disk space. Explorer shows a normal name and a normal size, and the actual content is not there.

For a person opening notes in Obsidian this causes no trouble, because opening one makes OneDrive fetch it. The AI and your scripts, though, sweep the folder and try to read dozens or hundreds at once. Then the downloads back up and it crawls, or it fails partway, or with no connection it cannot read at all.

The fix is one click. Right-click the vault folder and choose "Always keep on this device." It is the single most important action in this entire book.

Check that it took, and keep it from unravelling

Once you set it, the icon beside the folder changes. There is a surer method than memorizing icon shapes, though.

  1. Look at the size in the vault folder's properties

    If size on disk is close to zero, it has not come down yet. Even with hundreds of notes, text adds up to a few megabytes. That is no reason to hesitate over "always keep."

  2. Wait for syncing to finish

    With a lot of files it takes a few minutes. When the OneDrive icon in the taskbar reads "up to date," you are done.

  3. Disconnect from the internet and open a note

    This is the definitive test. Turn off wi-fi, open any note in Obsidian, and if it looks normal you have it.

Pitfall · the setting undoes itself

Windows has a feature that, when storage runs low, sends OneDrive files you have not touched in a while back to the cloud. It lives under storage in Settings. Leave it on and months later your vault quietly reverts.

The symptom is the worse part. It is not the whole vault. It picks off exactly the notes nobody has opened. So the AI's answers lose only your older material, and that is hard for a person to notice.

Two things to do. Re-apply "always keep" to the vault folder, and turn off the automatic storage cleanup. Add one line to the weekly check in Chapter 17: "is the vault folder about the same size as last week?" After that the check watches it for you.

Every new teammate means doing it again

"Always keep" is a setting on the PC, not on the folder. When someone joins or somebody swaps laptops, it has to be done once more on that machine.

So write the procedure into _ops/00-getting-started.md inside the vault, and put this item first in the onboarding document. Above "install Obsidian."

Stopping edit conflicts with operating rules

OneDrive will merge a Word document that two people edited at once. Plain text files like markdown it will not merge. When two people edit the same note separately, a copy appears beside the original. The name gets a marker showing who produced it, and the exact shape depends on your environment. The filename 1.md from Chapter 4 is one of those copies.

The copy appearing is not the disaster. The real problem is that nobody knows it appeared. Search in Obsidian and the same content comes back twice, and the AI reads the two as two different facts.

Technology cannot stop this. Rules can. Four are enough.

RuleConcretelyWhat it prevents
1. Give every axis an owner 10-Products/ is the CTO's, 30-Partners/ is Park's, 00-Company/ is Kim's Two people opening the same note becomes rare in the first place
2. Split the files that many people write to Worklogs get one file per date and person, like 40-Worklog/2026-07-30-kim.md Several people adding lines to one file stops happening
3. Edit someone else's note briefly Fix what needs fixing on the spot and close it. Do not walk into a meeting with it open Obsidian autosaves constantly, so time open is conflict probability
4. One person writes during a meeting Name the minute-taker as you start. Everyone else adds afterwards Removes the single biggest source of conflicts entirely

Rule 1 does the most work in practice. It looks like a file rule and it is really an organizational rule. When you set your classes in Chapter 9 you also settle "who owns this axis," and that decision gets used right here.

When a conflict copy already exists

Even with the rules, the first few weeks produce a handful. Deal with each one the day you find it. Put it off and nobody remembers which side is current.

  1. Open the two files side by side

    Obsidian has no automatic compare. It is text, though, so your eyes find the differing lines quickly.

  2. Merge by hand and save into the original

    Do not pick one of the two. Keep the content from both sides. Usually the two people edited different parts, so it merges cleanly.

  3. Delete the copy

    Leave it and it keeps poisoning the next search and the next AI answer.

  4. Check OneDrive version history if you need it

    Right-click the file on the web and you get a list of earlier versions. That is your safety net for a merge gone wrong.

Pitfall · the .obsidian folder conflicts constantly

The settings folder from Chapter 5 causes trouble here. Inside it is a file that records which tabs you currently have open. It differs per person and changes every time you open a note. Sync the whole vault and this one file conflicts once per teammate.

There is no tidy solution. There is also no actual damage, because that folder contains not one character of knowledge. Chapter 4 ruled out plugins, so it holds settings and a theme.

So treat it like this. Conflict copies inside .obsidian get deleted without reading them. Put it in your monthly tidy-up. Conflict copies inside Sources/, on the other hand, never get deleted unread. Those are knowledge.

Solbit Tech in practice: the first conflict

Day four after connecting, during preparation for the Daesung Logistics meeting. Park was writing the meeting schedule into 30-Partners/DaesungLogistics.md, and at the same moment Kim opened the same note to add a line reading "no contract yet, cannot be cited as track record."

A conflict copy appeared, and nobody noticed. Three days later, drafting a proposal, the AI mentioned Daesung Logistics twice: once as a track record, once as pre-contract. Two contradictory sentences came out of the same vault.

The four rules above were set that day. Conflicts effectively stopped once "Park owns the customer axis" was decided. When Kim has something to fix, Kim does not open the note. Kim tells Park. It costs one extra step, and in return who knows what gets sorted out inside the organization.

What must not go in the vault

The moment it becomes a shared folder, a new question shows up: "can this go in too?"

One test. The vault holds only what the whole team may see. You could split permissions folder by folder. Do that and the AI answers from half a vault while the person reading trusts the answer without knowing. An AI that sees half is more dangerous than an AI that sees nothing.

So instead of splitting permissions, put things outside the boundary.

MaterialIn the vaultDo this instead
Salaries, performance reviews, HR recordsNoLeave them in your existing HR system
National ID numbers, personal contact listsNoPerson notes carry a name and a role, nothing more
Passwords, API keys, certificatesNoUse a password manager. The vault says only "it lives there"
Source material a customer gave you under NDANoThe vault records that you received it, the date, where it is kept, and the usage limits
Original contractsUsually noMove only facts like period, funder, budget and due into a note
Unpublished technical work, filings in preparationYes, with a level on itWrite confidential: 대외비 in the frontmatter

That last row matters. Strip out everything sensitive and the vault becomes a shell. So you keep one layer that goes in with a level attached. The level scheme gets designed in Chapter 10 along with the relations and fields. For now, remember one field name: confidential.

What "goes in with a level attached" means

Rather than splitting folder permissions, you write the level into the note and have the AI read it. One line in the frontmatter does it.

---
class: 과제
id: PRJ-2025-SmartLogistics
status: 진행
confidential: 대외비
period: 2025-04 ~ 2026-03
funder: Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy
budget: 확인 필요
updated: 2026-07-20
---

That line on its own stops nothing. It is text in a file. It works once the matching rule goes into CLAUDE.md in Chapter 11. "Do not quote the contents of a note marked confidential: 대외비 in any external draft. If you need it, say only that the fact exists and ask a person to confirm."

The structure differs from folder permissions. Folder permissions make something invisible. This makes it visible but unusable. So the AI sees the whole vault and still answers "that project is confidential, I cannot use it here." Better than seeing half.

One condition comes with it. This is an after-the-fact rule, not a hard block. Same structure as the reason Chapter 4 deferred MCP. Material that genuinely must not leak stays outside the vault from the start, per the table above. Levels are a tool for the middle ground.

Three questions that settle it in thirty seconds

1. If this leaks, who gets hurt? If it is a person rather than the company, it does not go in.

2. May everyone on the team see it? If even one person may not, it belongs somewhere other than the vault.

3. May the AI summarize this into a proposal? This question works best of the three, because putting something in the vault means it may end up quoted in an external document some day.

The third question is what filters out the Daesung Logistics material. Daesung Logistics has no contract yet. Put the warehouse drawings and volume figures from that meeting into the vault, and months later the AI writing a different proposal pulls those numbers in as evidence. By then nobody remembers the material belonged to someone else. The moment something enters the vault it starts behaving like company knowledge.

Try it: connect the team and cause a conflict on purpose

The last item sounds odd, and meeting your first conflict on a safe note is far better than meeting it on a real one.

  1. Make the vault in the shared folder and connect at least two PCs. Alone, you cannot verify half of this chapter.
  2. Set "Always keep on this device" on both machines, then turn off wi-fi and open a note. That is the pass condition.
  3. Make a practice note and have two people edit different lines at the same time. Save on both sides and wait about five minutes. See with your own eyes, in your own environment, what the conflict copy gets named. The shape varies by environment, so this one has to be watched.
  4. Write the naming pattern you saw into _ops/00-getting-started.md. Then nobody panics the day that file turns up.
  5. Give every axis an owner. Write six folders on paper and a name beside each. If a folder gets no name, that folder probably is not needed yet.
  6. Write a five-line "not going in the vault" list with your team. Try to decide later and it is already in.

Before you move on to Chapter 7

  • The vault sits under OneDrive - CompanyName, and teammates see the same files on their PCs
  • Every PC has "Always keep on this device" applied, and notes open offline
  • Every axis has an owner, and worklogs are split into one file per person and date
  • You have a list of what does not go in the vault, written into _ops/00-getting-started.md

Quick recap

  • "Send it over" means the system has failed. One shared folder removes the copies
  • Use "Add shortcut to OneDrive" from the channel's Files tab. A short path means fewer typos in prompts and scripts
  • "Always keep on this device" is the most important click in this book. Skip it and the AI cannot read files you can plainly see
  • Verify by cutting the internet and opening a note. Surer than reading icon shapes
  • Automatic storage cleanup quietly reverses the setting. It takes only the notes nobody opened, which makes it hard to spot
  • Text files do not merge. Four rules hold it: an owner per axis, split files for shared writing, short edits, one writer per meeting
  • Delete .obsidian conflict copies unread. Never delete Sources/ conflict copies unread
  • The vault holds only what the whole team may see. Split the permissions and the AI answers from half a vault
  • Judge with three questions. Who gets hurt, may everyone see it, and may the AI summarize it into a proposal

Coming up

The folder has spread across the team. Time to attach the hands. You connect the vault to Claude Cowork and have a first conversation. Most people make the same mistake here. "I connected the folder" is not "the AI has read the vault." Miss that difference and when an answer comes back empty you will never find the cause.
