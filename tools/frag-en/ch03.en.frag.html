This conversation actually happened.

CEO

"You told me the ontology was finished. So now I can ask the AI about our track record, right?"

Knowledge lead

"No... there is nothing to ask it yet."

Neither of them is lying. They are using the same word to mean two different things. The CEO heard "ontology" as a finished company knowledge base. The knowledge lead meant a rules document that decides how notes get formatted.

The mix-up looks minor and it wrecks whole projects. The CEO thinks the work is done and moves on to the next thing. The knowledge lead now finds it hard to say "we still have to fill the notes." Three months later what is left is a vault with one design document in it.

That mix-up is why this chapter exists. It is the shortest chapter in the book and there is no new tool to learn. What you get instead is four layers that every later chapter keeps using.

Split it into four layers

Here is the same thing as a table. This table is the whole chapter.

LayerNameWhat it isAs filesHow often it changes
0 Taxonomy
classification		 The hierarchy of folders and tags. "Where does this note go?" Folder structure Almost never
1 Ontology
schema		 A schema. The definition of what kinds of things exist, how they connect, and which fields are mandatory One design document Once a quarter
2 Knowledge graph
notes and links		 Layer 1's format filled in with real company facts. Note bodies and links Hundreds of notes Every day
3 LLM Wiki Layers 0 to 2 plus AI rules, skills, scheduled jobs, checks and team habits: a system that runs The whole vault folder Constantly

In one sentence

The ontology is the blueprint, the knowledge graph is the building that got built from it, and the LLM Wiki includes how people and AI work inside that building.

Drawing a blueprint does not give you a building. Having a building does not mean work gets done inside it.

What each layer actually looks like

Words alone get confusing again, so look at the files. Take one fact: "Warebot uses object recognition, and that capability came out of a 2025 government project." Watch how it gets written down differently at each layer.

Layer 0 · Taxonomy: where a note goes

Sources/ ├── 00-Company/ company, org, capabilities, IP ├── 10-Products/ ← Warebot.md lives here ├── 20-Projects/ ← 2025-government-project.md lives here ├── 30-Partners/ ├── 40-Worklog/ └── 90-Reference/

Layer 0 tells you that Warebot is a product, and nothing more than that. A folder holds a note in exactly one place, so this alone is not enough. Warebot is a product and, at the same time, an output of a government project.

Layer 1 · Ontology: rules only, no facts

Part of _ops/01-ontology-design.md. This is the ontology.

## Classes

| class    | prefix | how you decide                     | required fields              |
|----------|--------|------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| 제품     | PRD-   | a unit you sell or deliver         | status, owner, since         |
| 기술역량 | CAP-   | a reusable bundle of technology    | trl, evidence                |
| 과제     | PRJ-   | has a contract or a budget         | period, funder, budget       |
| 기회     | OPP-   | no contract or budget yet          | stage, due                   |

## Relation predicates

| predicate      | direction        | meaning                   |
|----------------|------------------|---------------------------|
| pdm_uses       | 제품 → 기술역량  | tech this product uses    |
| pdm_output_of  | 기술역량 → 과제  | project that produced it  |
| pdm_owner      | any → person     | the owner                 |

## Constraints

- every note must carry the class, id and updated fields
- status is exactly one of: 기획 / 개발 / 운영 / 중단
- never write a number without evidence. write 확인 필요 instead

Check this yourself

The word Warebot never appears in that document. No revenue, no owner's name, no TRL number. The ontology knows nothing about your company. It knows only that a thing called a product exists, and that a product carries these fields.

Ask the ontology to "summarize our track record" and nothing comes back. It is not the kind of thing you ask.

# Warebot

Inbound and outbound automation software for logistics warehouses.
Running since April 2023.

## Known limits
- No recognition-rate data for freezer warehouses. 확인 필요

One line goes into Sources/00-Company/CAP-ObjectRecognition.md.

pdm_output_of: "[[PRJ-2025-SmartLogistics]]"

Those two links in those two files are two edges in layer 2. Now the question from earlier has an answer.

"Which technology in Warebot came out of a government project?" Warebot --pdm_uses--> CAP-ObjectRecognition --pdm_output_of--> PRJ-2025-SmartLogistics ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^ answer

The answer comes from layer 2. Layer 1 contributed nothing to it directly. But without layer 1 you would have written pdm_uses yesterday, uses_tech today and tech_used tomorrow, and the link would lead nowhere. That is layer 1's job.

Eight sentences people get wrong

You hear these all the time. When the sentence on the left leaves your mouth, the layers have blurred.

If you say thisWhy it is wrongSay this instead
"Ask the ontology and you get an answer" Layer 1 holds no facts "Read the notes and you get an answer"
"The ontology is finished" Usually only layer 1 is done "The ontology design is done, now we fill the notes"
"We designed the LLM Wiki" You design layer 1. Layer 3 you run "We designed the ontology"
"The ontology is growing" What grows is the notes in layer 2 "The notes are growing"
"A knowledge graph needs a database" Layer 2 works with markdown links "Write the links by the rules and you have a graph"
"We built an ontology out of tags" Tags are layer 0. They cannot express relations or constraints "We built a classification scheme out of tags"
"The ontology is so complex the AI gets confused" What the AI reads is layer 2 notes and layer 3 rules "There are so many required fields the notes never get filled"
"The wiki is built but the AI cannot answer" Usually layer 3 (rules and checks) is missing "We have no CLAUDE.md and no checks yet"

A simpler way to see it: try accounting

Layer 0 is how you divide up the filing cabinet. Layer 1 is the chart of accounts and the posting rules. It says nothing about how much money the company has. Layer 2 is the actual ledger, and the numbers live there. Layer 3 is the closing procedure, the checks, the month-end report, and the habit of taping in a receipt the day you get it.

Nobody asks whether the chart of accounts will tell them their revenue. The ontology is the same.

What happens when you have only one side

Two failures happen, and they are exact mirrors of each other. Both are common.

Solbit Tech in practice: the time they fell into A

In the first week Solbit Tech grew the class list to 19: product, capability, project, opportunity, org, person, patent, paper, trade show, award, certification, equipment, dataset, meeting, decision, risk, customer inquiry, hiring, regulation.

The design document was beautiful. Two weeks later they had written 6 notes. Every single note meant stopping to decide, "is this a paper or a dataset?"

In the end they cut the list down to 7. The rest moved down into fields or tags. Over the next two weeks 71 notes appeared.

You measure the quality of layer 1 by how well layer 2 gets filled, not by how refined it is. That sentence comes back in Chapter 9.

So which layer is RAG?

Lay the RAG comparison from Chapter 2 over this picture and it comes out like this.

RAG skips layer 2 and papers over it with search. That is why setup is fast, and that is why context breaks. An LLM Wiki has people build layer 2, which makes it slow and makes it accurate. The conclusion of Chapter 2 fits in that one table.

If the phrase knowledge graph put you off

Large companies put layer 2 in a dedicated graph database. You do the same job with one link line in a markdown file. Different scale means different tools. The layers are identical.

At a few hundred notes, files are the better side anyway. A person can read them and fix them directly.

Why use the hard word at all

Fair question. Why not call it "note rules"? You can. Inside your own team that may be the better name.

This book keeps the word "ontology" for three reasons.

  1. The AI understands this word

    Say "write this note following the vault's ontology" and Claude takes it as an instruction to check the classes, the relations and the constraints. That lands far more precisely than "follow the rules."

  2. It connects when you grow

    When the company grows and you start looking at a real knowledge management tool or a data catalog, the layer 1 you built maps straight onto their concepts. Build it in your own private vocabulary and you have to translate it all over again.

  3. It forces you to keep the layers apart

    Call it "note rules" and it blends back into the layer 3 operating rules. That is what happens in practice. The words have to differ for the layers to stay apart.

That said, you never have to use the word outside your team. When you report to your CEO, "we settled the note format and the linking rules" is plenty.

What this distinction is finally for

Splitting things into layers may look like academic taste. It is not. Whether AI really lands in your company is decided here.

You hear a lot about AX (AI transformation) these days. It means bringing in AI and changing how the company works, and most companies stall in the same place. Getting each person to draft email or summarize a document with AI goes fine. Past that, nothing moves.

Here is why, in terms of layers.

What the company hasWhat you can hand to AIWhat it cannot do
Nothing
no layer 0 to 2 at all		 General writing, summaries, translation Any question about your company. Ask and it invents something plausible
Documents gathered in folders
layer 0 only		 Hand it one file and it answers about that file Combining documents. Spotting contradictions. Saying "I do not know"
Only a design document
layer 1 only		 Nothing Everything. There is not a single fact
Notes piling up by the rules
layers 1+2		 Combining, cross-checking, citing evidence. Answering "no record" Nothing happens unless a person asks
Rules and automation too
layer 3		 You can hand over one step of a workflow Strategic calls about the company. Those stay with people

The gap between the fourth row and the fifth row is the line between a person using AI and a company working with AI. To cross that line you have to have layer 2.

Why layer 2 is the blocker: it comes down to review

Put AI into a workflow and someone has to check what comes out. To check it, they need to know where the claim came from.

With layer 2, the answer arrives together with the note it came from. Checking takes 30 seconds. Without layer 2 you get a conclusion only, and checking means digging through everything again. That is slower than doing the work by hand, and three weeks later someone turns the automation off.

That is why the book runs in this order. Layer 1 quickly (Part 3), layer 2 diligently (Part 4), automation on layer 3 (Part 5), and how all of it connects to your company's AX (Part 7, Chapter 19).

So, to put it together. The ontology is not the goal of AX. It is the precondition. And the LLM Wiki is not AX itself. It is the floor AX stands on. Everyone stalls in the same place because they try to raise walls without laying a floor.

Three questions to check yourself

Thirty seconds tells you whether your layers have blurred.

Can you answer these three right away?

  • How many files hold facts about your company? That number is the size of layer 2. Under 10 and you are still in state A.
  • Where is the file that lists your classes? If you cannot find it, you have no layer 1. What sits in your head is not layer 1.
  • Did anything run last week without a person asking for it? If not, you have no layer 3. What you have is a folder, not a system yet.

Try it: split one company fact across four layers

Ten minutes is enough. You need this instinct when you do the real design in Chapter 9.

  1. Pick one sentence your company uses often in proposals. (For example: "Our product A applies technology B and ships to customer C.")
  2. Circle the nouns. Write down what kind of thing each one is. A product, a technology, a customer. Those are your candidate layer 1 classes.
  3. Write down the verbs between the nouns. "applies," "ships to." Those are your candidate layer 1 relation predicates.
  4. Now split the sentence into three notes and write them out, with the values each note carries. That is layer 2.
  5. Finally, ask yourself: "what would it take to keep these three notes current six months from now?" Everything that comes out of that is layer 3.

Quick recap

  • Four layers: Layer 0 · Taxonomy, Layer 1 · Ontology, Layer 2 · Knowledge graph, Layer 3 · LLM Wiki
  • The ontology (layer 1) contains not a single company fact. Only definitions of classes, relation predicates, fields and constraints
  • Answers to questions come from layer 2. Layer 1 is the rule set that keeps layer 2 from falling apart
  • LLM Wiki (layer 3) is the name for the whole system: layers 0 to 2 plus AI rules, skills, scheduled jobs, checks and habits
  • "The ontology is finished" usually means only layer 1 is done. That mix-up is what stops projects
  • Failure arrives in mirror image. One is a designed but empty vault, the other is a scratchpad piled up without rules
  • You measure layer 1's quality by how well layer 2 gets filled, not by how refined it is
  • RAG skips layer 2 and papers over it with search. That is why it is fast, and why context breaks
  • The ontology is not the goal of AX, it is the precondition. To move from a person using AI to a company working with AI you need layer 2, because that is when review becomes possible
