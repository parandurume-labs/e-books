In Chapter 11 you wrote your company rules into CLAUDE.md. The AI now knows what your team must not do. But the vault is still nearly empty.

Week three of the Solbit Tech rollout produced this scene. Kim said "right, let's write the Warebot note first" and opened a new file. Twenty minutes later the screen held one heading, one sentence of description, and a cursor.

Not for lack of ideas. Kim could talk about Warebot all day. Kim stopped because there was no way to know how much to write before the note was finished.

What this chapter is for

Rules (Chapter 11) tell you what you must not do. Templates tell you what counts as done. Notes get written only when you have both.

In the layers from Chapter 3, a template is a piece of layer 3 machinery that turns the field definitions in layer 1 (the ontology) into a shape a person can actually fill in.

People stop in front of an empty note

Read a template as "a tool for consistent formatting" and you are half right. Consistent formatting is the third effect. The first two are much bigger.

What a template doesWhat happens without one
1. It sets an end
eight boxes means you are done after eight		 work with no visible end does not get started. Twenty minutes of blinking cursor
2. It makes the gaps visible
an empty box left over is a hole you can see		 nobody can tell what is missing from a blank page. You find out when you write a proposal
3. People and AI use the same format field names diverge, and every table and Gantt chart in Chapter 15 breaks

The second one is the heart of this chapter. An empty box labelled "basis for the TRL call" sitting in a note keeps nagging at you. With no box at all, the absence of a basis is itself invisible. The accident Chapter 1 recorded as "last year's document says TRL 4, this year's says TRL 6" starts here. There was no evidence box, so nobody asked for evidence.

Seven templates

The four backbone classes from Chapter 9 (제품, 기술역량, 과제, 기회) plus three operating formats (업무로그, 결정, 회의록) make seven templates. The support classes from Chapter 9, 조직 and 사람, get no template. They carry three or four fields, which is not worth freezing into a format. You are better off not making more than these.

This template has a different character from the other six. It is a note you seal, not a note you fill. Why go that far is the next section.

Do not make an eighth

You will want a 조직 template. Customer notes, like the one for Daesung Logistics. Do not make one at the start. An organization note carries three or four fields, which does not need a template.

Every template you add adds one more "which template is this?" moment. It is the same trap as Solbit Tech writing 6 notes in two weeks with 19 classes in Chapter 8. Freeze a format into a template after you have written three notes in it, not before.

Keep the source and the extract in separate files

The split is simple.

  • Source: anything you did not create, or created but must not edit. Meeting transcripts, a request for proposals you received, a customer's email, an outside report
  • Extract: whatever a person or AI pulled out of a source. Decision notes, updated fields on a product note, a newly created organization note
Sources/ ├── 40-Worklog/ │ ├── 2026-07-15-Park.md work log · extract │ ├── 2026-07-15-weekly-meeting.md meeting source · untouched │ └── DEC-2026-0715-JoinCityCall.md decision · extract └── 90-Reference/ ├── 2026-06-daesung-logistics-rfp.pdf received as it came ├── 2026-05-city-call-announcement.md the call, as published └── 2026-07-15-weekly-meeting-transcript.md transcript · untouched

Four reasons never to delete the source.

  1. When an extract is wrong, the source is the only place to go back to

    A strange sentence in a decision note means opening the source and comparing. With no source there is nothing to compare against, and that sentence stays a fact forever.

  2. Summarizing removes information. You learn what got removed later

    The line in a meeting source that says "they're worried about the recognition rate because it's a freezer site" looks trivial at the time. Eight months later, preparing a freezer warehouse project, it turns out to be the most important thing in the file. When you summarize you do not know what will matter.

  3. Evidence needs its own evidence

    Chapter 3 said layer 2 is what makes review possible, because notes supply evidence. Which leaves the question, what is the evidence for that note? The source is the answer. Go that far and the chain holds.

  4. Later it becomes the only material for checking a fact

    Contract negotiation memos, the spec a customer asked for, the terms of a project agreement. You go looking for these after a dispute starts. At that point "the summary we wrote" is not evidence.

Put these three lines into CLAUDE.md. Append them to the file you built in Chapter 11.

## Handling source material

- Never modify a note under Sources/90-Reference/, under any circumstances.
  Leave the typos, the broken sentences and the line breaks exactly as they are.
- Every note built from a source links back to it with source:.
- Never delete or move a source file. If it has to move, tell a person.

The most common accident: "tidy this up" overwrites the source

Open a meeting note file and say "tidy this up a bit," and the AI polishes the same file and saves it. It fixes typos, smooths the sentences and reorders things. It was trying to help.

At that moment the source is gone. And you do not notice it is gone, because the file name is unchanged. The fourth mistake in ⓕ of Chapter 16 is exactly this.

One way to stop it. Ask for the save and the extraction in one go, but ask for the save first. "Save the raw meeting note into 40-Worklog first, then pull out the decisions." Never reverse the order.

Where do meeting memos go?

One case sits on the boundary. The short memo you type yourself during a meeting. It did not come from outside, so putting it in 90-Reference feels off.

The test is "who made it." Anything from outside, and transcripts, go in 90-Reference/. A meeting note you typed is of a piece with the decisions pulled out of it, so it goes in 40-Worklog/ as a source note. The table above, recipe 1 in Chapter 16 and the folder diagram in Chapter 18 all follow that.

Either way, the principle is the same: keep the source note and the extract in separate files. Which folder matters less than whether there are two files.

One confidence box sets the trust of the whole vault

The confidence field settled in Chapter 11 looks like this in a note.

confidence: draft      # AI wrote it, or a person wrote it in a hurry and nobody has checked
confidence: reviewed   # a person read it and judged that it is still correct

The rules are already nailed into CLAUDE.md. So here you look at running it. Even with rules, this box dies easily.

The first job is for the team to settle what reviewed means, in one sentence.

The sentence to use

"I put my name on the facts in this note still being right."

Not "I read it." Not "looks about right." Leave this sentence unsettled and in three months reviewed comes to mean "a note somebody once scrolled past," at which point the box may as well not exist.

The person putting their name on it is the owner. That is why the two fields travel together. A note that is reviewed with an empty owner is a fact nobody is answerable for, and the check in Chapter 17 catches that combination.

This one box does work in four places.

Wheredraftreviewed
evidence in external documents (Chapter 16, recipe 2)not used. Goes onto the "needs verifying" listused as evidence
what automation may edit (Chapter 14)may be editedno field is touched
weekly dashboard (Chapter 15)the trend in the reviewed share becomes the vault's health metric
a person's to-do listthe draft list is the review queue as it stands

You can already guess what happens without this box. AI first passes become half the vault within months, and the next AI reads them as fact. Worse, people read them the same way. With no way to tell which facts were verified, you end up trusting none of it. Once that happens it takes months to undo, because every note has to be read again from scratch.

Stamping everything reviewed in the first month

It feels good. The vault looks clean. And at that moment the field dies.

There is a way to check. If the reviewed share is 100% or 0%, the box is not being used. There is no correct percentage. But a living vault always has some notes waiting for review.

reviewed is a fact as of the day it was checked. It does not last forever. The check in Chapter 17 pulls out a list of reviewed notes whose updated has gone stale.

Where templates live and how you call them

Outside Sources/. A template is a format, not a company fact.

_ops/ ├── 01-ontology-design.md ├── 02-terminology.md ├── scripts/ └── templates/ ← here ├── product.md ├── capability.md ├── project.md ├── opportunity.md ├── worklog.md ├── decision.md └── meeting-note.md

Put them inside Sources/ and empty shell notes turn up in search results and the graph view. Ask "how many products do we have?" and the answer is one too many. It sounds trivial, but this kind of thing piles up until you cannot trust the AI's answers.

Calling them in Obsidian

Obsidian ships Templates already. It is a core feature, not a community plugin, so there is nothing to install. As of July 2026 it works like this.

  1. Turn on "Templates" in Settings, Core plugins

    If it is not in the list, search for "Templates". The label varies with your language setting.

  2. Set the template folder to _ops/templates

    Settings, Templates, "Template folder location".

  3. Check the shortcut

    Call "Insert template" from the command palette. You will use it often, so a keyboard shortcut is worth setting.

Getting AI to use them

AI uses these templates more often than people do. Put three lines into CLAUDE.md.

## When making a note

- Always start a new note by copying the matching template from _ops/templates/.
- Never delete a box from the template. If there is nothing to put in it, write 확인 필요.
- The only places you may create files without human approval are _reports/ and _moc/.
  When making a note under Sources/, show a table first and get approval.

The second line gets ignored most often. AI treats empty boxes as untidy and tries to remove them. Remove the empty boxes and the second effect from this chapter is gone. What is not written stops being visible.

When you change a template, do not go back and apply it

Around the three-month mark you will want to add a box to a template. When you do, do not rewrite every existing note into the new format. Two things blow up at once. Touching hundreds of files at the same time triggers the OneDrive sync conflicts from Chapter 6, and every note you touch has to drop back to draft, so the review queue explodes.

Apply it from the next note you make. Old notes get fixed the next time you work on them anyway. A vault is not somewhere you tidy in one pass. It is somewhere that improves a little as you walk through it.

Building one first note end to end

Let me build the Warebot note from start to finish. Six steps.

  1. Decide which class it is

    The class test splits it. "Is it a unit you sell or deliver externally?" If yes, it is 제품. Warebot is in delivery talks with Daesung Logistics and already runs at three sites, so it is 제품.

  2. Copy the template

    Create Sources/10-Products/Warebot.md and paste in the product template.

  3. Fill the frontmatter top to bottom

    For a box you do not know, do not leave it empty. Write 확인 필요. Left empty, there is no telling whether it was skipped or genuinely absent.

  4. Three paragraphs of body is plenty

    Trying to write it perfectly means not writing it. One paragraph of "what you sell and to whom," the installed-sites table, and the list of known limits. That is your first version.

  5. Add the links

    Write wikilinks like [[CAP-ObjectRecognition]]. It is fine if that note does not exist yet. Obsidian shows it in grey. The list of grey links is the list of notes to make next.

  6. Set the confidence

    If you checked it yourself as you wrote, reviewed. If you wrote from memory, draft. When unsure, draft.

Solbit Tech in practice: 40 minutes on the first note

Kim took 40 minutes to finish the Warebot note. Thirty of those went into working out whether there were three installed sites or four. Asking Park, digging through the contracts folder, and finally concluding that one of them was a pilot and could not be counted as track record.

Those 30 minutes looked wasted, but in fact those 30 minutes were the entire value of the system. Until then, "four sites" and "three sites" had gone out in different proposals and nobody had noticed.

The second note took 12 minutes and the tenth took 4. The cost of checking is front-loaded. You do not check a fact twice once you have checked it.

And the Warebot note built that day went straight into the proposal draft in recipe 2 of Chapter 16. As "three sites."

Three ways templates fall apart

1. Too many boxes

While designing a template they all look necessary. But the more required boxes there are, the fewer notes get written. It is exactly the trap of the 19 classes in Chapter 3.

Do not go past five required boxes. Around class, id, status, owner, updated. Leave the rest optional and allow 확인 필요. You measure a template's quality not by its number of boxes but by how many notes get written.

2. Everyone uses a different template

It starts with "I'll just tweak mine a little." Two months later owner, assignee and responsible are all in play. Then the table in Chapter 15 grows three columns, and asking "what is the load per person" gets you a wrong answer.

Templates live in exactly one shared folder. When something needs changing, change that file and tell the team. The moment people make their own copies, the controlled vocabulary collapses.

3. You changed the template and not CLAUDE.md

The quietest failure of all. Add a box to a template while the field list in CLAUDE.md stays as it was, and notes people make and notes AI makes drift into different formats. No error appears. You find out two months later on the dashboard.

Templates, CLAUDE.md and _ops/01-ontology-design.md are one body. Get into the habit of opening the other two whenever you change one. You can also make the check script in Chapter 17 catch drift between the three.

Five things to check after your first note

  • Are class, id and updated all in the frontmatter? Those three are required in every note.
  • Is there at least one 확인 필요? If there is none, you probably filled it in loosely. It is hard for a first note to contain nothing you are unsure of.
  • Can you see grey links? Those are the notes to make next. None at all means this note connects to nothing.
  • Did you set confidence deliberately? Leaving the template default is not the same as making a call.
  • Did you set a confidentiality grade? Pre-contract customer names, unit prices, labour costs. These are the hardest items to find and fix later.

Try it: one product note for your company, end to end

If you do one thing from this chapter, do this. Block out 40 minutes. Half of it goes into checking.

  1. Pick one product. Not the one you know best. Pick the one you put in proposals most often. That way this note goes to work immediately.
  2. Copy the product template and create a new note in Sources/10-Products/.
  3. Fill the frontmatter. Write 확인 필요 in the boxes you do not know. Do not leave them empty.
  4. You will get stuck at least once filling in the installed-sites table. Do not stop there. Actually go and check. Open the contracts folder or ask whoever owns it. These 30 minutes are the most valuable time in this chapter.
  5. Write at least two lines under "known limits." If you think there are none, it means you have not sold this product enough yet.
  6. Save the note and count the grey links. That number is how many notes you have coming.
  7. Last, ask Claude. "Write the company introduction paragraph for a proposal using only this note. Do not write anything that is not in the note." How thin that paragraph comes out is the state of your vault right now. Do not be discouraged. It is a one-note vault.
